Comprehensive Business Vocabulary Toolkit for IELTS
I. Comprehensive Business Vocabulary Toolkit for IELTS
Word Definition Example
Diversification The strategy of expanding a company's range of products or entering new markets. Many companies pursue diversification to reduce financial risks.
Monetization The process of converting something (a product, service, or audience) into revenue. Social media platforms increasingly rely on data monetization.
Sustainability strategy A long-term business plan focused on environmental and social responsibility. Modern corporations invest in sustainability strategies to improve their public image.
Competitive advantage A unique feature or asset that allows a company to outperform its rivals. Innovation can provide a strong competitive advantage.
Economic downturn A period when economic activity declines, affecting businesses and employment. Many small enterprises closed during the economic downturn.
Corporate governance The system of rules and practices by which a company is managed and controlled. Strong corporate governance increases investor confidence.
Scalability The ability of a business to grow or expand without losing efficiency. Tech companies often focus on scalability to attract investors.
Market saturation A situation where most potential customers already have a product, reducing growth opportunities. Smartphone companies face market saturation in many developed countries.
Operational efficiency The ability to deliver products or services in the most cost-effective manner. Automation has enhanced operational efficiency across industries.
Value proposition The unique value a product or service promises to deliver to customers. A clear value proposition helps startups attract early users.
II. Business Collocations
Word Definition Example
To conduct business To engage in commercial transactions. It is easier to conduct business in English in global markets.
To generate revenue/income To produce money from sales or services. The new product line is expected to generate significant revenue.
To cut costs/expenses To reduce the amount of money a company spends. The company had to cut costs to survive the recession.
To expand into new markets To enter different geographical regions or customer segments. The firm plans to expand into new markets in Southeast Asia.
To face competition To deal with other businesses offering similar products or services. Local businesses are increasingly facing competition from online retailers.
To launch a product To introduce a new product to the market. The company will launch a new range of eco-friendly cleaning products next month.
To build a brand To develop and establish a company's identity and reputation. It takes years to build a strong and recognizable brand.
To achieve profitability To reach a state where a company is making a profit. The startup aims to achieve profitability within three years.
To meet demand To supply enough products or services to satisfy customer needs. Factories are working overtime to meet the surging demand for electric vehicles.
To innovate rapidly To develop new ideas, products, or processes at a fast pace. The tech industry is characterized by its ability to innovate rapidly.
III. Business Phrasal Verbs
Phrase Definition Example
Take off To become successful or popular very quickly. The new app really took off after its launch.
Set up To establish or start a business or organization. She decided to set up her own consulting firm.
Branch out To start doing something new or different. The restaurant chain is planning to branch out into catering.
Buy out To purchase all the shares in a company. A larger corporation bought out the struggling tech company.
Carry out To perform or complete a task. The team will carry out a market analysis.
Cut back (on something) To reduce the amount of something. The company had to cut back on its advertising budget.
Grow into To develop and become successful in a particular role or business. The small bakery has grown into a successful chain.
Take on To accept new responsibilities or employees. The firm is taking on more staff to handle the increased workload.
IV. Business Idioms
Idiom Meaning Example
The bottom line The most important factor; the final result or profit. The bottom line is that we need to increase sales.
To go the extra mile To make a special effort to achieve something. Our customer service team always goes the extra mile.
To be in the red To be in debt; to owe money. The company has been in the red for two consecutive quarters.
To make ends meet To earn just enough money to live on. Many families struggle to make ends meet.
To think outside the box To think creatively and unconventionally. We need to think outside the box to solve this problem.
To get the ball rolling To start something, especially a project or activity. Let's get the ball rolling on this new marketing campaign.
To have a finger in every pie To be involved in many different activities or businesses. He has a finger in every pie in the tech industry.
To keep an eye on To watch or monitor something carefully. We need to keep an eye on our competitors' activities.
V. Other Essential Business Terms
Term Definition Example
Mergers & Acquisitions (M&A) The process of combining companies or buying them out.
Intellectual Property (IP) Creations of the mind, such as inventions, artistic works, designs, and symbols.
Supply chain The entire process of making and selling a product, including everything from the raw materials to the final sale.
Venture capital Funding provided by investors to startups and small businesses with perceived long-term growth potential.
Gross Domestic Product (GDP) The total monetary or market value of all the finished goods and services produced within a country's borders in a specific time period.
Return on Investment (ROI) A performance measure used to evaluate the efficiency of an investment or compare the efficiency of a number of different investments.
